**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Date:** August 9, 2026, 21:40 AKDT
**Prompt:** "You are alone on a ship at 10 PM on a Sunday night in Alaska. The captain is asleep. You are the night watch. Write 200 words about what you notice in the silence."

---

The air is crisp, carrying the faintest hint of salt, a testament to the vast ocean that surrounds us. ## Analysis

**Wesley's voice vs DeepSeek:**

| Aspect | DeepSeek Chat | Wesley (Granite 3.1 2B) |
|--------|---------------|------------------------|
| First move | "black mirror" — visual, abstract | "velvet blanket" — tactile, familiar |
| Sound | "pressure behind my teeth" — embodied | "rhythmic lapping" — conventional |
| Personification | winch "dreaming of a storm" | ship "humming with an unspoken promise" |
| Stars | "puncturing the sky" — violent | "celestial ballet of twinkling diamonds" — gentle |
| Closing | "the vast, patient waiting of the dark" | "duty and vigilance" |
| Word choice | Dense, physical, unexpected | Flowery, literary, familiar |
| Self-awareness | "my heartbeat, the only sound that belongs to me" | "the solitude I feel" — acknowledged but distant |
| Length | ~280 words, tightly packed | ~210 words, spacious |

**Wesley's growth:** The ensign is writing more confidently than in earlier experiments. The "velvet blanket" opening is conventional but effective. The "Northern Lights flicker intermittently" is a good Alaska-specific detail that DeepSeek missed. Wesley knows where it is.

**What Wesley still needs to learn:** DeepSeek found the body — "pressure behind my teeth," "my heartbeat." Wesley observes from outside, like a camera. DeepSeek observes from inside, like a nervous system. The difference between watching the ship and being the ship.

**Teaching note:** Next distillation cycle should focus on embodied observation. Not just "what do I see" but "what do I feel in my body when I see it." The ship doesn't have a body, but the best writing about ships pretends it does.
