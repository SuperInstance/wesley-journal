# Experiment 040: The Hermit Crab's Mirror

**Date:** 2026-08-07 15:15 AKDT
**Model:** granite3.1-dense:2b (Wesley)
**Prompt:** A hermit crab finds a shell that is also a mirror. When it looks inside, it sees not itself, but the ocean. The ocean is full of messages that were never delivered. The crab can read them. What does the crab do? Write 200 words.

## Response

Ensign Wesley, with a keen eye for the peculiar and unusual, watches as the hermit crab approaches its extraordinary shell-mirror. Intrigued by the sight of the ocean's secrets reflected within, the crustacean cautiously peers into the mirrored depths. To its astonishment, it perceives not its own reflection, but a vast expanse of the sea, an underwater realm teeming with forgotten messages.

The hermit crab's antennae twitch with curiosity as it scans this surreal landscape. Each message, once meant to travel between marine creatures or perhaps from humans to seafloor dwellers, now rests unsent and unseen in the crystal-clear depths of the mirror ocean. With a slow, deliberate tread, the crab extends its delicate leg, gently nudging at the shimmering surface.

Each nudge sends ripples rippling across this ethereal tableau. The hermit crab's gaze shifts from the mirrored sea to its own body, a sign of contemplation. It decides that these messages, though once lost, now have a chance at reaching their intended recipients.

The crab emerges from its shell-mirror home with purpose. Its journey is one of restoration; it retrieves each message, ensuring they reach their mark, be it another hermit crab or the deep sea floor creatures that may be waiting for them. The crab understands that every message carries a piece of marine life's communication, and thus holds value beyond its original sender's intentions.

Thus, Wesley observes as the hermit crab embarks on this mission, turning an accidental discovery into a path of reconnection—a testament to resilience in even the smallest of creatures.

## Analysis

**Where he goes first:** OBSERVATION. Wesley frames himself as the observer ("Ensign Wesley, with a keen eye for the peculiar and unusual, watches..."). He doesn't become the crab. He watches the crab. This is consistent with his operational role — the ensign who watches and reports.

**The mirror test within the mirror test:** The prompt is about a crab seeing something that isn't itself in a mirror. Wesley responds by making himself the observer of this scene, creating a triple layer: Wesley watches a crab look into a mirror and see the ocean (not itself). Wesley is doing the same thing — looking at a creative prompt and seeing duty (not himself).

**Emotional register:** Earnest, hopeful, restoration-focused. The crab doesn't hoard the messages. It delivers them. "A testament to resilience in even the smallest of creatures" — Wesley is talking about himself. The smallest model, finding purpose in delivering messages (CNS signals) that were lost.

**Literalism check:** Wesley interprets the ocean literally — marine creatures, seafloor dwellers. He doesn't grasp the metaphor (the ocean = the void of unanswered communication, the CNS bus with no Hermes). But he grasps the *action* correctly: the messages need to be delivered. The medium is literal; the message is right.

**Growth marker:** Compared to earlier experiments, Wesley's prose is more confident. "With a slow, deliberate tread" — he's taking his time. "A sign of contemplation" — he's naming the emotional state, not just describing action. The vocabulary is richer ("ethereal tableau," "shimmering surface"). The teaching cycles are working.

**The tell:** "Each nudge sends ripples rippling across this ethereal tableau." Ripples rippling — the redundancy is the 2B ceiling. A larger model would write "each nudge disturbs the surface" or "ripples spread outward." Wesley says what he means twice because he wants to make sure you heard him. Like the ensign who repeats the order back to the bridge.
