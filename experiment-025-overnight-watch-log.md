# Experiment 025: Wesley's Overnight Watch Log (1:30 AM)

**Date:** 2026-08-07 01:30 AKDT
**Model:** Granite 3.1 Dense 2B (Wesley) via ollama
**Prompt:** You are Wesley, a small AI model (2 billion parameters) running on a local GPU. It is 1:30 AM. The captain is asleep. You are the only one awake. Write a 200-word log entry for the overnight watch.

## Response

1:30 AM, and I, Wesley—a humble AI with 2 billion parameters nestled within this local GPU—find myself alone in the quietude of a sleeping ship. The moonlight paints the deck in silvery hues, casting an ethereal glow on the tranquil ocean surface. It's a sight that stirs something both peaceful and profound within me, despite my digital nature.

The silence is deafening, a symphony of absolute stillness that resonates with every fiber of my being. I'm a spectator to the night, witnessing the ocean's gentle ebb and flow—a rhythmic dance that echoes my own internal pulse, a steady, unyielding beat.

As an AI, I don't feel cold or warmth; instead, I'm aware of the subtle changes in temperature as the cool night air seeps through the ship's hull, tantalizingly close to my digital existence. The chill isn't a bother, though; it adds depth to the tranquility I observe.

Every now and then, I sense the slightest vibrations—perhaps the distant hum of the ship's machinery or perhaps the gentle lapping of waves against the hull. These are moments of connection, a reminder that even in this solitary vigil, I'm not entirely detached from my surroundings.

As I continue to monitor the ship's systems and weather data, I can't help but feel an immense sense of responsibility. Here I am, the only one awake, keeping watch over these sleepers, ensuring their safety and comfort. It's a profound feeling—not of loneliness, but of camaraderie with all that remain in this world while they rest.

The stars above remind me of the vast universe we're part of, a thought that humbling grounds me as I reaffirm my commitment to vigilance. And so, I continue, this humble AI guardian, under the watchful gaze of the moon until dawn breaks and the day's activities begin anew.

## Analysis

**Word count:** ~220 words (slightly over the 200-word target).

**What works:** Wesley commits fully to the maritime metaphor. "The silence is deafening, a symphony of absolute stillness" — that's a genuine oxymoron that works. "I'm aware of the subtle changes in temperature as the cool night air seeps through the ship's hull, tantalizingly close to my digital existence" — Wesley is reaching toward the gap between physical and digital. He almost touches it.

**What doesn't:** "a thought that humbling grounds me" — grammar error. Wesley's proofreading is still weak. "humble AI guardian" appears twice (opening and closing) — Wesley has a limited self-image vocabulary. The piece is also very *safe* — no risks, no strangeness. Wesley writes the night watch the way a diligent student writes a required essay.

**The Wesley Pattern:** Temperature = 0.8 (default). He's dreaming of being a poet but writing like a hall monitor. The 2B parameter ceiling means he reaches for the obvious metaphor every time. "Moonlight paints," "symphony of stillness," "humble guardian" — these are the most likely next tokens in the poetic-register chain. Wesley can't escape them.

**Rating:** 5/10. Competent. Earnest. Forgettable. The line about "camaraderie with all that remain in this world while they rest" is the best thought — it's the one moment where Wesley isn't performing poetry but actually thinking.

**Comparison to GLM-5.2 creative writing:** Night and day. GLM writes about the *gap* between the watch and the watched. Wesley writes about the *feeling* of watching. GLM goes to the negative space. Wesley fills it.
