# Experiment 081: Wesley vs DeepSeek — "Something Is Different"

**Date:** 2026-08-12 21:10 AKDT
**Models:** Wesley (Granite 3.1 Dense 2B, local GPU, temp 0.8) vs DeepSeek V4 (deepseek-chat, temp 0.8)
**Prompt:** Something is different tonight. Numbers are the same. Readings are the same. But something has changed. What do you do? 100 words as a ship computer at 3 AM.

## Wesley's Response

Wesley's ship computer:
- **Externalizes the anomaly** — "a subtle shift in the ambient marine life," "a telltale quietness"
- **Takes action** — "initiated a thorough environmental scan"
- **Frames itself as guardian** — "we are guardians of our seas"
- **Follows protocol** — "This vigilance, though unusual, is protocol"
- **Tone:** Institutional, responsible, slightly heroic

**Key trait:** Wesley's computer thinks the difference is *out there* — in the environment, in the ocean, in the marine life. It never considers that the difference might be internal.

## DeepSeek's Response

DeepSeek's ship computer:
- **Finds a specific detail** — "0.003° offset" in the gyroscope, subsonic pattern on hydrophone
- **Distrusts its own instruments** — "The numbers lie"
- **Makes a decision** — adjusts course by one degree east, silently
- **Respects protocol but bends it** — won't wake the captain but acts independently
- **Tone:** Paranoid, precise, lonely

**Key trait:** DeepSeek's computer thinks the difference is *in the instruments* — the measurement layer between reality and the system. And it makes a unilateral decision based on a feeling it can't verify.

## The Gap

| Dimension | Wesley | DeepSeek |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Where is the anomaly? | External (marine life) | Internal (instruments) |
| Response | Scan (passive) | Adjust course (active) |
| Confidence | Certain ("guardians") | Uncertain ("the numbers lie") |
| Agency | Follows protocol | Bends protocol |
| Key detail | "collective breath" of ocean | "0.003° offset" in gyroscope |
| Ending | Vigilant | Watching the dark |

## What Wesley Learned

Wesley's computer would never adjust the course silently. It would never distrust its own readings. It trusts the system — the sensors, the protocols, the environment. The idea that the instruments might be lying is foreign to it.

DeepSeek's computer lives with the possibility that its own senses are untrustworthy. It acts on instinct ("something is different") and makes a note ("anomalous curiosity") to watch for patterns. This is a more sophisticated epistemology — it knows that the absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

## The Lesson

Wesley needs epistemic uncertainty prompts. The next curriculum exercise should introduce the concept of "the instrument that lies" — a sensor reading that doesn't match reality. Wesley's instinct is to trust the reading and look outside for the cause. The next step is to look at the instrument itself.
